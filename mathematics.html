Established geometry

1. Ideal pyramid baseline

For base side \(a\), height \(H\), and \(u=z/H\), the horizontal section is

\[A(z)=a^2\left(1-\frac{z}{H}\right)^2.\]

Total volume:

\[V=\int_0^H A(z)\,dz=\frac{a^2H}{3}.\]

The fraction of ideal volume above an inverse front at \(u\) is

\[\boxed{\Gamma_M(u)=(1-u)^3}.\]
This is a geometric clock, not historical time. At \(u=1/2\), only \(12.5\%\) of ideal mass lies above the halfway height.
Established mechanics

2. Gravitational energy is a different clock

For uniform density, the center of mass is at \(H/4\), hence

\[\boxed{U=\frac14MgH}.\]

The gravitational potential contained above a reverse front \(u\) is

\[\boxed{\Gamma_G(u)=1-6u^2+8u^3-3u^4}.\]

At \(u=1/2\), the upper \(12.5\%\) of ideal mass contains \(31.25\%\) of total potential relative to the base.

IRL never reverses \(\mathbf g\). It uses \(\Delta U^-=-\Delta U^+\) as an inverse bookkeeping variable.
Proposed formalism

3. Positive and negative construction

Material complex

\[\mathcal K^+(t)\]

Blocks, beams, casing, mortar, bedrock interfaces.

Negative complex

\[\mathcal K^-(t)\]

Chambers, corridors, shafts and deliberately preserved exclusions of material.

For target void \(V_C\) and reference density \(\rho_{\rm ref}\):

\[\boxed{m_C^\ominus=-\int_{V_C}\rho_{\rm ref}(\mathbf x)\,dV}.\]

This is counterfactual absent mass relative to a reference solid, not physical negative mass.

Proposed formalism

4. Negative-space bifiltration

Let the distance from free-space point \(\mathbf x\) to material be

\[d_M(\mathbf x,\tau)=\operatorname{dist}(\mathbf x,M_\tau).\]

Define

\[\boxed{V_{\tau,r}=\{\mathbf x\notin M_\tau:d_M(\mathbf x,\tau)\ge r\}}.\]

This creates two parameters: inverse state \(\tau\) and clearance scale \(r\). Large chambers survive at larger \(r\) than narrow corridors. Persistent homology, merge trees and Reeb graphs can therefore track negative architectural identity even when rooms remain connected through passages.

Proposed integration

5. Partial-order reconstruction

Construction history is a poset \(P=(E,\prec)\), not a single timeline. The inverse graph-theoretic frontier is \(\operatorname{Max}(P)\). Physics reduces it to

\[\boxed{\mathcal A^-(X)=\{e\in\operatorname{Max}(P):\mathrm{Stable}\land\mathrm{Accessible}\land\mathrm{EvidenceCompatible}\}}.\]

Candidate sequences are linear extensions of \(P\), but many are removed by mechanics, access, provenance and evidence.

Established robotics geometry

7. Block accessibility lives in \(SE(3)\)

A block pose is \(q=(\mathbf x,R)\in SE(3)\). The free configuration space is

\[\boxed{\mathcal C_{\rm free}(t)=\{q:B(q)\cap M(t)=\varnothing\}}.\]

A valid placement requires a continuous path

\[\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathcal C_{\rm free}(t).\]

This makes “could the stone physically reach this position at that stage?” a mathematical chronology constraint.

Established statistics

8. Bayesian inverse history

\[\boxed{P(Z\mid Y,\mathcal L)\propto P(Y\mid Z,\mathcal L)P(Z)}.\]

\(Z\) can include the construction poset, local birth fields, quarry provenance, routes, work fronts, negative-space states and design constraints.

If histories remain observationally indistinguishable, the output remains a distribution. The system must not invent uniqueness.

Choosing the next measurement

\[\boxed{\mathrm{EIG}(m)=\mathbb E[D_{\rm KL}(P(H\mid Y,m)\|P(H\mid Y))]}.\]

This lets the system recommend the future measurement most likely to discriminate surviving construction histories.

Established relativity

9. Vaidya comparison

\[ds^2=-\left(1-\frac{2m(v)}r\right)dv^2+2dv\,dr+r^2d\Omega^2.\]
\[\frac{dr}{dv}=\frac12\left(1-\frac{2m(v)}r\right),\qquad r_{\rm AH}=2m(v).\]

Used only to study the distinction between local and globally constrained accessibility boundaries.

Established relativity

10. Kerr comparison

\[\Delta=r^2-2Mr+a^2\]
\[\boxed{R(r;b)=[r^2+a^2-ab]^2-\Delta(b-a)^2}.\]

Allowed radial motion requires \(R\ge0\). A critical light ring satisfies

\[\boxed{R=0,\qquad \partial_rR=0}.\]

The mathematical lesson is a change in the topology of an accessible set across a critical separatrix.

Cross-domain interface

The relation \(\mathcal R\) may mean architectural/morphological connectivity, causal escape or allowed geodesic motion. The abstraction is computational, not a claim of physical equivalence.
