# Theory: Inverse Reality Ontology

## 1. Terminal-state inversion

The laboratory begins at inverse time `tau=0`: the instant immediately after completion of the object. For Khufu, the desired terminal state is the reconstructed **as-built** pyramid rather than its modern eroded state.

Forward:
`Conception -> Design -> Construction graph -> As-built object`

Inverse:
`As-built object -> admissible predecessor states -> construction graph -> design constraints -> conception`

## 2. Positive and negative objects

A block is a positive material object. A chamber is not merely "air"; it is a deliberately preserved exclusion of material.

- `K+`: material complex.
- `K-`: negative/topological complex of architectural voids.
- `M+`: physical mass.
- `M⊖`: counterfactual absent mass relative to a specified solid reference.

`M⊖` is **not gravitational negative mass**.

## 3. Existence phases of a chamber

A chamber can have distinct events: volume reservation, wall emergence, closure, structural completion and encapsulation. Under inverse evolution these disappear in reverse partial order. The space can remain geometrically empty after it ceases to be an architectural object because it has reconnected to the exterior.

## 4. Causal cones of construction

For an entity `e`, its forward dependency cone `D+(e)` contains later entities or operations that depend upon it. In inverse disassembly, `e` is not eligible for removal while relevant descendants remain.

Dependencies are typed: structural, geometric, access, load, void-preservation, material/provenance and procedural.

## 5. Accessibility as a unifying mathematical abstraction

A complement-defined object is

`N_R(lambda) = Omega \ Accessible_R(Omega, lambda)`.

The relation `R` may mean material connectivity, causal escape, or allowed motion in phase space. This creates a mathematical comparison between architectural cavities, trapped causal regions and geodesic forbidden bands without asserting physical identity.

## 6. Epistemic requirements

Every inferred transition should carry evidence references, uncertainty, alternative histories, identifiability status and predicted observable consequences. A model is useful only if it can be falsified by new archaeological, geological, structural or muographic evidence.
