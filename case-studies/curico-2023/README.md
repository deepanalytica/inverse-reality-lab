# Curicó 2023 — Cascading Hydrogeomorphic Event Observatory

**Status:** evidence-building / reproducible case study
**Lab:** Inverse Reality Lab
**Region:** Curicó–Mataquito system, Maule, Chile
**Events:** June 21–26 and August 18–23, 2023

## Research question
How did extreme meteorological forcing propagate through hydrogeomorphic, infrastructure and social systems across the Curicó/Mataquito territory, and what role did antecedent land-cover/use conditions play in the spatial pattern of impacts?

## Scope
This case treats the 2023 disaster as a **cascading phenomenon**, not isolated incidents. Current nodes include Upeo–Monte Oscuro, Potrero Grande, Cordillerilla/Los Niches, Camino Las Piedras/San Martín de Las Piedras, Chequenlemu, Estero Guaiquillo, Parque Zapallar, Dragones Sur, Sarmiento and the wider Lontué–Teno–Mataquito network.

## Epistemic rule
A shared storm is not proof of direct causal connectivity. Every edge is classified as **documented**, **supported inference**, **hypothesis**, or **rejected**. This repository does not assert that the Upeo debris flow caused flooding in Zapallar; they may be separate branches of the same regional event.

## Evidence layers
MapBiomas Chile 1999–2024; DEM/drainage; rainfall/temperature/freezing level/discharge; Sentinel/Landsat; MOP/Vialidad/SENAPRED/DGA/municipal records; geolocated media; repair contracts.
